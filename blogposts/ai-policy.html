My Generative AI Policy

My Policy on Generative AI in writing:

The culture is getting to a point in which it will be considered necessarily for all authors disclose AI usage and to what purpose. Thus I will share my views, policies and uses of generative AI in my work.

AI generated writing:

I do not allow AI tools to draft any of my work, nor do I accept its edits or phrasing suggestions. This is my personal preference, due to the tendency of AI to consistently revert to the statistically most likely phraseology, leading to cliche ridden writing with a flattened voice. However, I have no wish to judge others who do accept AI edits or allow AI generated content into their writing. Each individual has to do what works best for them. I simply prefer to do all the drafting, myself. It’s a form of therapy for me, and I find the discovery of interesting, but less common synonyms to be a titillating exercise.

AI editing:

I generally use generative AI for assisted self edits in the earlier phases of a revision cycle. I find it tends to be very good at spotting problems. AI tends to be less good at finding solutions, but once I know the problems and inconsistencies that need to be worked on, I tend to find once I sit and have a brew while thinking about it, I can imagine some pretty good solutions, myself. I find AI can be particularly good with inconsistent spellings of made up names, looking for plotholes, and seeking out repetitive phrases. It should be used with extreme caution when it comes to suggestions on how to phrase sentences however, because AI errs to the side of stripping too much away, leaving a shallow husk of what was once an example of idiosyncratic prose.

AI art:

I am an artist, not as a profession, but as a passion. I’ve spent years learning how to draw, paint, sculpt, create digital works with both raster and vector graphics, pixel art, and 3D modelling. I like the struggle of creating something and having full control of how it’s made. I typically use AI for the initial concept and for minor elements that I then modify or recreate to my liking. I find having a visual reference of what I want helps me know what imagery I’m aiming for when I create it. I strive to have no AI generated elements in the final product of my book cover.

AI in other promotional materials:

I do not allow Generative AI tools to draft posts, tweets, essays, or content on my webpage, nor do I let draft emails for email lists. Just as with writing my fiction, I see this as my responsibility. I do consult with ChatGPT for brainstorming marketing ideas as well as the code for my website.

Other Uses:

I use Generative AI for things like brainstorming and fleshing out ideas. I find when I have a vague concept, simply the act of prompting and seeing my own ideas thrown back at me in a more organized fashion can give me an idea as to where I’m going with a concept, and helps me flesh them out. I will also use AI for early research, especially when it’s a quick “What would be most likely in this situation…?” kinds of research. I generally conduct varying levels of fact checking depending on how essential it is for the information I receive to be completely accurate vs simply something plausible. For a space opera, a plausible scenario might be fitting, so I might just look up some quick bits on an online encyclopedia. For historical accuracy, I may look up books and first hand sources to make a plot point as historically accurate as possible.

My writing:

I’m not bothered if my writing is used for AI training data, because, in my view, training is not the same as copying and plagiarizing. If I were to read someone’s books, ane then proceed to write my own works with similar ideas, phrases, etc., having been influenced by those books, I would not be stealing. AI does something similar when it trains and returns an output. AI models forget the training data when done with training and only come away with statistical data, and, when prompted, it comes up with what is the most statistically likely outcome. In short, I do not use AI in the final product of anything I create. All uses are behind the scenes.
